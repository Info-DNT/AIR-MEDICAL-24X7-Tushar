How to Arrange an Emergency Air Ambulance From Philippines: Step-By-Step Guide

November 27, 2026

When a patient needs to be moved urgently, the first call usually happens before anyone has the full picture. The family may not have the medical report. The treating doctor may still be in surgery. Somebody has to start the process anyway.

Knowing it helps twice over: you can prepare the right information early, and you can tell whether the provider you are speaking to is working the case properly or simply quoting a price.

How is an Emergency Air Ambulance Arranged?

An emergency air ambulance is arranged by sharing the patient's condition with a medical transport provider, who assesses fitness to fly, confirms a receiving hospital, selects the aircraft and medical team, arranges permits and ground ambulances, and then transfers the patient bed to bed. Timing depends on medical stability, documentation and operational clearances.

Step 1: Assess the Patient's Condition

Ask the treating team how the patient is right now: the diagnosis in the hospital's own words, whether the patient is in the ICU, conscious, on oxygen or a ventilator, and whether the condition is stable, improving or deteriorating. If the patient is unstable, the answer may be that the transfer waits. 

Step 2: Contact a Medical Transport Provider

The first call is short. Have ready the patient's name and age, the current hospital and city, the diagnosis as you understand it, the intended destination, and a contact number that stays reachable. You do not need every answer before calling.

Step 3: Share The Medical Information

The medical team needs a current clinical picture. The flight physician needs a current clinical picture while the medical team needs a current clinical picture. Ask the ward for a report covering:

  1. Diagnosis and date of admission
  2. Latest vital signs: heart rate, blood pressure, oxygen saturation, respiratory rate
  3. Level of consciousness
  4. Oxygen or ventilator settings, if applicable
  5. Medications and infusions currently running
  6. Recent laboratory and imaging results
  7. Any recent surgery or procedure, with dates

A photograph of the chart is normally enough to begin. Expect the flight physician to ask for a call with the treating doctor. That conversation usually saves hours.

Step 5: Coordinate With The Treating Hospital

Some procedures are safer completed in hospital than in the air, and the treating physician decides this. The family's job is practical: make sure the hospital knows a transfer is planned and that billing or discharge formalities will not block departure.

Step 6: Confirm The Receiving Facility

The receiving hospital should confirm acceptance and bed availability before the transfer proceeds. The receiving hospital reviews the report, confirms a bed in the appropriate unit and names an accepting physician. Where the family has no preference, the provider can approach facilities with the relevant specialty. This normally runs in parallel with flight planning.

Step 7: Arrange Ground Transportation

Ground ambulances may be needed at the departure and arrival ends. Ground ambulances may be needed at the departure and arrival ends. In Metro Manila, traffic between hospital and airport can add significant time, so pickups are scheduled around it. For patients outside major cities, reaching the departure airport may involve a long road journey or a connecting flight.

Step 8: Confirm The Aircraft and Flight Arrangements

The operator selects an aircraft suited to the route and the medical requirement, then applies for landing, overflight and slot permissions. Aircraft availability, crew duty limits, airport operating hours and weather all affect the schedule, so departure times cannot be guaranteed before these are in place.

Step 9: Complete the documentation

International transfers generally require a valid passport for the patient, passports and visas for accompanying relatives, destination entry requirements where applicable, the medical report and transfer summary, fit-to-fly documentation, consent from the patient or next of kin, and insurance authorisation where an insurer is paying.

Where a commercial flight is used, the airline's medical clearance applies. Philippine Airlines requires a Medical Information Form completed by the attending physician, submitted five to seven days before travel, with stretcher requests made at least 48 hours in advance. Other airlines set their own rules.

Step 10: Transfer The Patient

The medical team takes a handover from the ward, examines the patient, moves them onto transport equipment with lines and drains secured, and travels by ground ambulance to the aircraft. Monitoring continues throughout the flight. On longer routes the aircraft may stop to refuel, and the patient normally stays on board with the team.

Step 11: Bed-to-Bed Coordination and Handover

A ground ambulance meets the aircraft at the destination. The medical team travels with the patient to the receiving hospital and gives a verbal and written handover to the accepting physician, including a record of the patient's condition during the journey.

How Long Does It Take?

Three things set the timeline: how quickly complete medical information arrives, how fast the receiving hospital accepts, and how long clearances take on the route. No responsible provider guarantees a departure time before the assessment, hospital acceptance and clearances are complete.

What Affects The Cost?

Quotations are case-specific. The main variables are origin and destination, distance and routing including fuel stops, the patient's condition and level of care, aircraft type, medical team composition, equipment and oxygen, ground ambulance at both ends, airport handling, permits and clearances, and urgency. Ask for a written quotation listing inclusions and exclusions before confirming.

FAQs

What information do I need before calling an air ambulance provider?

The patient's name and age, the current hospital and city, the diagnosis as the hospital describes it, the intended destination, and whether the patient is in intensive care or on oxygen or a ventilator.

How quickly can an air ambulance depart from the Philippines?

It depends on the medical assessment, documentation, hospital acceptance and operational clearances. Domestic transfers are usually quicker to arrange than international ones. Specific timings should be confirmed case by case.

Can an air ambulance land at a small provincial airport?

It depends on the airport, the aircraft and the applicable permissions. Where a suitable airport is not available nearby, the plan may combine ground transport with the flight.

Does the patient need to be stable to fly?

The treating physician and the transport medical team assess stability together. Some patients are advised to wait until their condition settles. Others are transferred precisely because they need care that is not available locally.

Can a family member travel on the aircraft?

This depends on the aircraft, the medical configuration and the patient's condition. Confirm it when the quotation is issued.

Do we need to pay before the flight?

Payment terms vary by provider and by whether an insurer is involved. Ask about this in the first call so it does not delay departure.
